# Photometric Filter systems
---
**Wide-band filter:** samples an interval of amplitude $\sim \left[400, 1000\right] \,\, \AA$ 
**Medium-band:** $\sim \left[70, 400\right] \,\, \AA$ 
**Narrow-band:** $< 70 \,\, \AA$ 

---
The **wide-band filter** systems that are currently used have, as prototype, the UBV system of Johnson & Morgan (1953, ApJ 117, 486)

## Johnson-Morgan UBV filter system
Approximate central [[Wavelengths EM Spectrum]] and bandwidths are:

| Band | $< \lambda > ( Angstrom )$ | $\Delta\lambda \,\, ( Angstrom )$ |
| ---- | -------------------------- | --------------------------------- |
| U    | 3600                       | 560                               |
| B    | 4400                       | 990                               |
| V    | 5500                       | 880                               |

![Untitled](Untitled%2026.png)

**Motivation:** for stars, (U-B) gives gravity, (B-V) gives temperature.

Later, this system was extended into the redder wavebands (R and I, till ~10 000 Å), including the near-infrared (JHK: 1.0-2.4 μm) and mid-infrared (LMN: till 10 μm) by too many teams using different filters (and detectors), which resulted in a set of highly confused and inconsistent definitions (especially in R and I).

Only “recently” has an effort been made to relate the different filter systems. 
The "master" reference is: Bessell M.S. (2005, ARA&A 43, 293)

## Thuan-Gunn filter system

The Thuan-Gunn filter system (1976, that originated ugriz bands) is similar to the UBVRI system but, in this case, the filters were optimized to detect faint galaxies, avoiding regions of sky lines; this system was used in the Sloan Digital Sky Survey (SDSS; [http://www.sdss.org/](http://www.sdss.org/)).
